Environmental Law & Land Use

Protecting property owners navigating New York's environmental landscape

Overview

Environmental issues affect property owners, businesses, and communities in ways that are often invisible — until they become legal problems. A flood zone reclassification, a buried oil tank, or a wetlands designation can stop a real estate transaction cold, trigger agency enforcement, or expose a property owner to significant liability.

Ward & Rafter helps clients navigate the intersection of environmental law, regulatory compliance, and land use. Whether you are facing a DEC enforcement action, dealing with contamination discovered during a real estate transaction, or seeking permits to improve your property, our attorneys provide practical, results-oriented counsel grounded in a deep understanding of New York's regulatory environment.

Environmental Challenges Facing Staten Island & Long Island Homeowners

Homeowners on Staten Island and Long Island face a distinct set of environmental pressures shaped by geography, aging infrastructure, and decades of residential and industrial development. These are not abstract concerns — they show up in your basement, your backyard, your closing table, and your insurance premium.

Coastal Flooding & Flood Zone Regulation

The aftermath of Superstorm Sandy fundamentally changed the regulatory landscape for coastal homeowners. FEMA flood zone remapping placed thousands of Staten Island and Long Island properties into higher-risk designations, triggering mandatory flood insurance requirements and, in many cases, costly elevation mandates. Homeowners seeking to rebuild, renovate, or sell must navigate a complex web of federal, state, and local requirements — including elevation certificates, substantial improvement rules, and DEC tidal wetlands permits. We help property owners understand their obligations, challenge improper designations, and pursue the approvals needed to move forward.

Underground Oil Tank Liability

Hundreds of thousands of homes across Staten Island and Long Island were heated by underground oil storage tanks, many of which were abandoned in place decades ago and never properly decommissioned. A leaking tank can contaminate soil and groundwater, triggering mandatory reporting to the New York State DEC and costly remediation obligations. These issues surface most often during real estate transactions, when a tank sweep reveals contamination that neither buyer nor seller anticipated. We represent property owners in DEC remediation proceedings, negotiate cleanup agreements, and advise on disclosure obligations and liability allocation in real estate transactions affected by tank issues.

Contaminated Soil & Groundwater

Proximity to Superfund sites, former industrial properties, and brownfields is a reality for many homeowners in our region. Vapor intrusion — the migration of underground contaminants into indoor air — is an increasingly recognized health hazard that can affect properties far from the original source. We help homeowners understand their rights when contamination originates off their property, engage with state and federal agencies, and pursue claims against responsible parties where appropriate.

Wetlands & Tidal Restrictions

New York State's tidal and freshwater wetlands laws impose strict limits on development near protected areas. DEC jurisdiction can extend hundreds of feet from a wetland boundary, restricting what homeowners can build, clear, or fill on their own land. Army Corps of Engineers permits add another layer of federal oversight. Many homeowners discover these restrictions only after purchasing property or when seeking to make improvements. We assist clients in obtaining DEC and Army Corps permits, appealing permit denials, and understanding the limits of regulatory jurisdiction over their property.

Stormwater & Drainage Disputes

Flooding from neighboring properties, inadequate municipal drainage, and disputes over stormwater flow are among the most common environmental complaints from homeowners in our area. When one property owner's improvements redirect water onto a neighbor's land, legal remedies may be available. We handle nuisance and trespass claims arising from stormwater and drainage issues, as well as disputes with municipalities over infrastructure failures.

How We Can Help

DEC Permit Applications & Appeals

Obtaining permits from the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation can be a lengthy and complicated process. We assist property owners in preparing and submitting permit applications, responding to agency requests, and appealing denials or conditions that are unduly burdensome.

Environmental Due Diligence in Real Estate Transactions

Environmental issues discovered at the closing table can derail transactions or expose buyers to unexpected liability. We advise buyers, sellers, and lenders on environmental due diligence, review Phase I and Phase II assessments, and negotiate contractual protections where contamination or regulatory issues are present.

Defense of Environmental Violations & Agency Enforcement

Receiving a notice of violation from the DEC or another environmental agency is serious. We represent property owners and businesses in agency enforcement proceedings, negotiate consent orders, and litigate contested violations where the agency's position is not supported by the facts or the law.

Land Use & Zoning Approvals

Developing or improving property in New York requires navigating local zoning codes, variance procedures, and, in many cases, environmental review under SEQRA. We guide clients through the land use approval process, represent them before zoning boards and planning commissions, and challenge improper agency determinations.

Neighbor & Nuisance Disputes

Environmental conditions on one property can have real consequences for neighbors. Whether the issue is contamination, stormwater, noise, or odor, we represent property owners in disputes where environmental conditions are affecting the use and enjoyment of their land.
