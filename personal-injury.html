New York No-Fault & Serious Injury

New York is a no-fault insurance state, which means your own insurance covers certain medical expenses and lost wages regardless of who caused the accident. However, to pursue a claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering, you must meet the threshold for a "serious injury" under New York law. We evaluate every case carefully to determine whether that threshold is met and advise our clients accordingly.

Slip & Fall Lawyer: Staten Island Premises Liability

Property owners in New York have a legal duty to maintain safe conditions for visitors, customers, and in some cases, passersby. When they fail to do so, whether through neglect, inadequate maintenance, or failure to warn of a known hazard, and someone is injured as a result, they can be held liable.

Slip and fall cases on Staten Island arise in a wide range of settings: grocery stores and shopping centers, restaurant and retail floors, apartment building common areas, construction sites, and public sidewalks maintained by the City of New York or private property owners. Each setting comes with its own legal rules and procedural requirements, and the window to take action can be narrow, particularly when a municipality is involved.

Common Causes of Slip & Fall Accidents

• Wet or slippery floors without adequate warning
• Uneven pavement, broken sidewalks, and cracked curbs
• Ice and snow on walkways, stairs, and parking lots
• Poor lighting in stairwells, parking lots, and hallways
• Defective stairs, handrails, and escalators
• Debris or obstacles in walkways
• Unmarked hazards in stores and commercial spaces

What You Should Do After a Slip & Fall

If you are injured in a slip and fall, document everything you can: photograph the condition that caused your fall, get the names of any witnesses, and seek medical attention immediately. Report the incident to the property owner or manager and request a copy of any incident report. Then contact an attorney as soon as possible. Evidence disappears quickly, and notice requirements in New York can be strict, especially when a government entity is involved.
