NYC's New Security Guard Wage Law Reaches Condo and HOA Communities

Not Just Office Buildings: What Local Law 61 of 2026 Means for Boards

New York City has enacted a new wage law for private security guards, and condominium and homeowners association boards that use security staff should take note. Local Law 61 of 2026 was enacted on January 29, 2026, after Council Int. No. 1391-A passed, and it sets minimum pay, paid time off, and benefit standards for security guards working across the city. The law takes effect 180 days after enactment, with the wage floor itself beginning January 1, 2027.

Coverage Depends on the Guard, Not the Building

The most important thing for a condo or HOA board to understand is how this law decides who is covered. It does not define coverage by building type, square footage, or unit count. Instead, the Administrative Code defines a "covered security guard employer" as any person who employs one or more "covered security guards" in New York City. A covered security guard is someone principally performing guard functions under the General Business Law who holds a current registration card under Article 7-A of that law.

That means the trigger is the guard's licensing status and the employment relationship, not whether the guard works in a commercial office tower, a residential condominium, or a gated community with a guardhouse. A homeowners association that staffs its own gate or contracts with a security firm for patrol services can be a covered employer just as easily as a commercial landlord.

What the Law Requires

Once a board or its security vendor is a covered employer, the law phases in three sets of obligations.

The minimum wage requirement begins January 1, 2027. The Department of Consumer and Worker Protection has published the initial rate schedule, which runs through June 30, 2027 and steps up partway through that period:

Guard Classification Jan 1 – Apr 30, 2027 May 1 – Jun 30, 2027
Armed guard $33.70/hour $34.65/hour
Unarmed guard, certified* $22.20/hour $23.15/hour
Unarmed guard, 0–36 months or new hire $18.87/hour $19.68/hour

*The certified unarmed rate applies to guards with 12 months of employment plus FDNY F-01, F-03, and F-04 certifications, FDNY Building Evacuation Supervisor training, and 2 hours of customer service/professionalism training.

Overtime applies at 1.5 times the regular rate after an 8-hour day or 40 hours in a work week. DCWP is expected to publish updated rates for the second half of 2027 and to continue updating the schedule going forward. Employers must pay whichever rate is in effect when the guard actually performs the work, regardless of when a contract was signed.

Minimum paid time off follows on January 1, 2028. A supplemental benefits requirement follows after that, on January 1, 2029, which employers can satisfy through benefits, cash, or a combination of the two.

A Narrow Carve-Out for Certain Tax-Incentive Housing

The law exempts employers already subject to prevailing wage requirements tied to specific real property tax incentive programs, along with government employers and a handful of other existing prevailing wage statutes. This carve-out is aimed at developments already receiving certain tax benefits in exchange for wage commitments. It is not a general residential exemption, and most condominium and HOA associations will not qualify for it.

Why Boards Should Pay Attention Now

Many associations do not employ guards directly. They contract with a security firm for a doorman, concierge, or patrol staff. That arrangement does not put the compliance question entirely on the vendor. A board negotiating or renewing a security services contract should expect vendors to build the new wage and benefit costs into their pricing well before the January 2027 deadline, and should ask directly how a vendor plans to comply.

Boards currently budgeting for the next fiscal year, or renewing a multi-year security contract, have a real reason to raise this now rather than after the wage floor takes effect.
